There's a particular kind of trust that builds between a kid and a dog — the kind where a dog
will rest its whole head against a child's face and just stay there. It doesn't happen by
accident. It's built slowly, out of hundreds of small, calm moments.

## A few things that help

- **Let the dog approach first.** Kids naturally want to run up and hug — most dogs would rather
  be greeted on their own terms.
- **Teach gentle touch early.** Soft strokes along the back, not pats on the head or hugs around
  the neck.
- **Respect mealtime and sleep.** No matter how bonded they are, a dog eating or sleeping should
  be left alone.
- **Always supervise.** Even the gentlest, most patient dog is still a dog, and young kids can't
  always read the warning signs.

Do this consistently, and you get exactly the kind of friendship in the photo above — a dog that
chooses to be close, and a kid who's learned to let it happen on its own terms.
