Rabbits get handed to kids as an "easy" pet more often than almost any other animal, but real
rabbit care is more involved than the stereotype suggests. They're social, surprisingly
expressive, and can live 8–12 years — closer in commitment to a dog than to a hamster.

## The basics that actually matter

- **Diet is mostly hay.** Unlimited grass hay should make up the bulk of what a rabbit eats,
  supplemented with fresh greens and a small amount of pellets — not just carrots and lettuce.
- **They need space to move.** A cramped hutch isn't enough; rabbits need daily time to hop,
  stretch, and explore outside of it.
- **Handle them low and secure.** Rabbits have fragile spines and powerful back legs — always
  support the hindquarters, and let nervous rabbits stay close to the ground.
- **They're social animals.** Many rabbits do better in bonded pairs than living alone.

Once the basics are in place, rabbits turn out to have real personalities — some are cuddly, some
are bossy, and most of them will absolutely let you know which is which.
